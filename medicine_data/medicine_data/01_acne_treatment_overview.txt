Acne vulgaris may involve comedones (blackheads or whiteheads), inflamed bumps and sometimes deeper lesions. Similar-looking facial changes may have other causes; a photograph alone is not enough for a diagnosis.

Treatments described in dermatology guidance include benzoyl peroxide, topical retinoids (such as adapalene), salicylic acid, azelaic acid, and selected clinician-directed prescription therapies. Choice depends on diagnosis, severity, skin sensitivity, medication history, pregnancy status and other context.

Benzoyl peroxide is used for acne and can cause dryness, stinging, peeling or irritation. It can bleach fabric or hair; avoid contact with eyes and damaged skin.

Topical retinoids help with comedonal acne but may cause dryness or irritation. Topical retinoids should not be used during pregnancy or when planning pregnancy; professional guidance is important.

Salicylic acid is used in some acne products to exfoliate and address blocked pores. Excessive combinations of exfoliating or active products may worsen irritation.

Azelaic acid is another acne treatment discussed in clinical references; possible side effects include temporary burning, itching, dryness or redness.

Prescription topical or oral antibiotics and isotretinoin are clinician-managed treatments, not automatic suggestions from Mateo. Antibiotic stewardship is important.

Basic non-medicated care: gentle cleansing, avoiding picking or abrasive scrubbing, and using suitable non-comedogenic products. Seek professional assessment for persistent, scarring, severe, painful, or worsening lesions.
